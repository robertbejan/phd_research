# Examples of Markov Processes

A Markov Process is a stochastic process for which at every step we take into consideration only the present state.
It can easily be described like a random walk. Statistically speaking, the past and the future are independent.

The random walk can be decribed by the following sum of random variables with mean 0 and variance 1.
X_N = sum(zeta_n), X_0=0

The diffusion process is described as a markovian process because the future states do not depend on the past, but just on the present state.

The following equation describes the discrete-time Markov process (called Markov chain):
P(X_t+h = i_t+h | {X_s, s <= t}) = P(X_t+h = i_t+h | X_t)
The following equation describes the continuous-time Markov process:
P(X_t+h in SIGMA | {X_s, s <= t}) = P(X_t+h in SIGMA | X_t)

You can also define the conditional probability densities for the Brownian motion and for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

The Markov property enables us to obtain an evolution equation for the transition probability:
P(X_n+1 = i_n+1 | X_n = i_n), P(X_t+h = i_t+h | X_t=i_t)
This equation is called the *Chapman-Kolmogorov equation*

The transition matrix P = {p_ij} is a stochastic matrix with nonnegative entries and sum(p_ij) = 1, and we can define the n-step transition matrix P_n = {p_ij(n)} as
p_ij(n) = P(X_m+n = j | X_m = i)

And we can study the evolution of a Markov chain through the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation:

p_ij(m+n) = sum(p_ik(m) * p_kj(n))

To get the transition matrix after 2 steps we will do P*P and note it as P^2

To further study the evolution we can consider a distribution vector mu_t:

mu_t = mu_o * P_t, where mu_0 is the vector distribution at time - and P_t is the transition matrix at time t.
